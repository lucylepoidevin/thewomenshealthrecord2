### High-sensitivity cardiac troponin I and T

- **Units**: ng/L (SI, now standard for hs assays). pg/mL is numerically identical (1 ng/L = 1 pg/mL). Older contemporary assays reported µg/L or ng/mL (1 ng/mL = 1000 ng/L).

- **Typical lab reference range**: hs-troponin is not reported as a "normal range" but as a 99th-percentile upper reference limit (URL), which differs by assay and often by sex.
  - University of Iowa Hospitals pathology handbook, "Troponin T, High Sensitivity" (Roche electrochemiluminescence, US 5th-generation): "19 years+ … Males =<22 ng/L … Females =<14 ng/L"; "1 years – <19 years … =<14 ng/L / =<11 ng/L". Sex-specific: yes. https://www.healthcare.uiowa.edu/path_handbook/rhandbook/test3665.html [S9]
  - NHS Lothian (Edinburgh) laboratory test directory, "Troponin T (high sensitivity)": "Males >16 years: 1-16 ng/L", "Females >16 years: 1-9 ng/L", "Males & Females, 6 months - 16 years: 1-14 ng/L". Sex-specific: yes. https://edinburghlabmed.test.certus-tech.com/node/1955 [S10]
  - Quest Diagnostics "Troponin I, High Sensitivity" (test 34693): a search summary showed a single range "≤47 ng/L" with no sex split, but the page is JavaScript-rendered and could not be read directly — **unverified** [snippet]. Mayo Clinic Laboratories and ARUP pages returned HTTP 403 and could not be read.
  - Manufacturer package-insert 99th percentiles, as tabulated by the IFCC Committee on Clinical Applications of Cardiac Bio-Markers (C-CB), v05/2023 [S5] (overall / female / male, ng/L):
    - Abbott ARCHITECT STAT High Sensitive Troponin-I (OUS insert G5-6634/R01, April 2015): **26.2 / 15.6 / 34.2**; US FDA 510(k) insert Sept 2019 (K2 EDTA): **28 / 17 / 35**.
    - Roche Elecsys cTnT-hs (cobas e601/e602/e411, EU 2022): **14 / 9 / 17**; Roche cTnT Gen 5 STAT (US 2022, plasma heparin): **19 / 14 / 22**.
    - Siemens Atellica High-Sensitivity TnI (TnIH): **45.4 / 38.6 / 53.5**; ADVIA Centaur TNIH: **46.5 / 39.6 / 58.0**; Dimension VISTA TNIH: **58.9 / 53.7 / 78.5**.
    - Beckman Coulter Access 2 hsTnI (OUS, Sept 2017; US LiHep plasma June 2018): **17.5 / 11.6 / 19.8**; US serum: 18.2 / 11.8 / 19.7; DxI LiHep: 17.9 / 14.9 / 19.8.
    - Ortho VITROS hs Troponin I: 11 / 9 / 12 (serum).

- **Where that range came from**:
  - Abbott: reference population "n = 1531, 21-75y; F: n=764, 21-75y; M: n=766, 21-73y"; statistic "Robust"; 85% of normals measured ≥ LoD overall (F 78%, M 92%) [S5].
  - Roche EU: "n=533, 20-71y", 57.4% ≥ LoD overall, 49.7% in women; statistic "NP" (not provided). Roche US Gen 5: "n = 1301, 21-89y", 55.1% ≥ LoD overall, 50.4% women [S5].
  - Siemens Atellica: "n = 2001, 22-91y; F: 1007; M: 994", non-parametric; 75% ≥ LoD (F 62%, M 89%) [S5].
  - Beckman Access: "n=1089, 21-99y; F: n=595; M: n=494", non-parametric; ">50%" ≥ LoD [S5].
  - The IFCC table's footnote: "All data have been listed as provided by the manufacturer … manufacturers may have submitted assays they claim to be 'high sensitivity' that do not meet the IFCC requirements of: a) <10% CV at the 99th percentile and b) ≥ 50% measurable concentrations ≥ LoD for both males AND females." [S5]
  - Independent re-derivation: Apple et al. 2020 (Clin Chem) used the AACC Universal Sample Bank, "426 men and 417 women, screened using a health questionnaire", ages 19–91, with surrogate exclusions "Hemoglobin A1c (>URL 6.5%), NT-proBNP (>URL 125 ng/L) and eGFR (<60 mL/min)" plus statin use, leaving n = 694 (354 men, 340 women). Post-exclusion non-parametric 99th percentiles (overall / men / women, ng/L): Abbott Architect **19 / 20 / 13**; Beckman Access 2 **18 / 18 / 20**; Siemens Atellica **38 / 44 / 26**; Siemens Centaur 39 / 40 / 26; Siemens Vista 60 / 68 / 44; Ortho Vitros 15 / 16 / 5; Roche cobas e602 hs-cTnT **16 / 16 / 10**; e601 16 / 17 / 11. The percentage of healthy women with measurable values was 42% (Abbott), 48% (Beckman), 21% (Roche e602), 16% (Roche e601): "The Roche cTnT and Beckman and Abbott cTnI assays (after exclusions) did not measure cTn values at the limit of detection in 50% women." The authors note "the surrogate exclusion criteria used to define normality tended to lower the 99th percentiles." [S6]
  - The Fourth Universal Definition of MI notes "Screening of apparently healthy subjects with imaging has been shown to lower the observed 99th percentile URL value" and requires that a hs assay "measure cTn values above the assay's LoD in ≥ 50% of healthy individuals" (Section 24) [S4].

- **Official sex-specific thresholds**:
  - Fourth Universal Definition of Myocardial Infarction (ESC/ACC/AHA/WHF, 2018), Section 25 "The 99th percentile upper reference limit": "Significantly lower values are observed among women compared with men" and "sex-specific 99th percentile URLs are recommended for hs-cTn assays". The same section adds: "sex-specific cut-off values have been reported to improve diagnostic and prognostic information" and "there is controversy as to whether this approach provides valuable additional information". [S4]
  - The values themselves are assay-specific (table above): e.g. Abbott 16 vs 34 ng/L (as used in High-STEACS), Roche 9 vs 17 ng/L (EU) or 14 vs 22 ng/L (US Gen 5), Beckman 11.6 vs 19.8 ng/L, Siemens Atellica 38.6 vs 53.5 ng/L [S5].

- **What is contested** (the High-STEACS evidence):
  - **Shah et al. 2015, BMJ** (doi 10.1136/bmj.g7873): 1,126 consecutive patients with suspected ACS (504 women, 622 men), Edinburgh. Contemporary Abbott ARCHITECT troponin I with a single threshold of 50 ng/L versus the ARCHITECT hs-cTnI with a generic 99th centile of 26 ng/L and sex-specific thresholds of **16 ng/L (women) and 34 ng/L (men)**. Type 1 MI in women rose from **11% (55) to 22% (111)**; in men from **19% (117) to 21% (131)**. **56 women** and 14 men were reclassified. At 12 months, death or reinfarction occurred in **25%** of reclassified women, 24% of women with MI by both assays, and 4% of women without MI. Reclassified women were treated far less than women with MI on both assays: cardiology referral 20% vs 80%, coronary angiography 2% vs 47%, PCI 0% vs 29%, aspirin 57% vs 85%. [S1]
  - **Shah et al. 2018, Lancet (High-STEACS trial)** (doi 10.1016/S0140-6736(18)31923-8): stepped-wedge cluster-randomised trial, 10 Scottish hospitals, **48,282** consecutive patients (47% women), June 2013–March 2016. Contemporary assay threshold 40 ng/L (seven sites) or 50 ng/L (three sites); hs-cTnI sex-specific thresholds 16 ng/L women, 34 ng/L men. "The high-sensitivity assay reclassified 1771 (17%) of 10 360 patients with myocardial injury or infarction who were not identified by the contemporary assay." Of the 1,771 reclassified, **1,470 (83%) were women** (vs 41% women among those identified by the contemporary assay). "In those reclassified, subsequent myocardial infarction or cardiovascular death within 1 year occurred in 105 (15%) of 720 patients in the validation phase and 131 (12%) of 1051 patients in the implementation phase (adjusted odds ratio for implementation vs validation phase 1·10, 95% CI 0·75 to 1·61; p=0·620)." Interpretation: "Use of a high-sensitivity assay prompted reclassification of 1771 (17%) of 10 360 patients with myocardial injury or infarction, but was not associated with a lower subsequent incidence of myocardial infarction or cardiovascular death at 1 year. Our findings question whether the diagnostic threshold for myocardial infarction should be based on the 99th centile derived from a normal reference population." Reclassified patients were less likely to have type 1 MI (515 [33%] vs 4513 [60%]). [S2]
  - **Lee et al. 2019, JACC (High-STEACS sex-specific sub-analysis)** (doi 10.1016/j.jacc.2019.07.082): 48,282 patients (22,562 women, 25,720 men). With sex-specific thresholds, myocardial injury in women rose from **3,521 (16%) to 4,991 (22%)**, a **42%** increase; in men from 5,068 (20%) to 5,369 (21%), a 6% increase — about five times more additional women than men identified. One-year MI or cardiovascular death in reclassified women vs men: 13% (195/1,470) vs 14% (41/301), HR 0.93 (0.52–1.68). For all women with myocardial injury, the primary outcome was 18% (369/2,072) before vs 17% (488/2,919) after implementation, adjusted HR 1.11 (0.92–1.33); for men 18% → 15%, aHR 0.85 (0.71–1.01). Coronary angiography in women with injury rose from 18% to 26%, new antiplatelet 29% → 34%, new statin 11% → 16%, but "women with myocardial injury remained less likely than men to undergo coronary revascularization." [S3]
  - **How many labs actually use sex-specific cut-offs**:
    - England (Goodacre et al., Emerg Med J 2020): survey of all 131 English acute NHS Trusts with >10,000 emergency admissions, 125 (95%) responded, 110 (88%) used a hs assay (Roche 57%, Abbott 14%, Beckman 11%, Siemens 11%). "The threshold for positivity was reported by 97/110 Trusts, with 79 using the 99th percentile (54 sex-specific, 25 overall), 12 using a higher threshold and six using a lower threshold." So **54 of 97 (56%)** Trusts reporting a threshold, or 54/110 (49%) of hs users, used sex-specific cut-offs in 2019. [S7]
    - International, outside the US (Howell et al., Crit Pathw Cardiol 2018): 18 institutions in nine countries; "Institutions employing the hs-cTnI assay favored sex-specific cut-offs (5/6, 83%) while institutions using the hs-cTnT assay favored a combined cut-off (12/12, 100%)." The authors attribute this to "the hs-cTnT manufacturer, Roche, releasing only a combined cut-off for institutions outside the US." [S8]
    - United States: no laboratory survey of sex-specific cut-off adoption could be opened. Apple et al. 2020 state that "For the Roche hs-cTnT assay, laboratories predominantly use an overall 14 ng/L 99th percentile URL [outside the US], while early adopters of the same assay in US hospitals are using sex-specific URLs" [S6]. A 2015 US survey of 159 laboratories (59% using the 99th percentile, pre-hs era) and a figure of "18% of hs sites" using sex-specific thresholds were seen only in search snippets — **unverified**.

- **What a reader with a value inside the contested band is told by the guidelines**: For a woman whose value lies between the female and male 99th percentile (e.g. 16–34 ng/L on Abbott, 9–17 ng/L on Roche EU), the Fourth Universal Definition says: "Detection of an elevated cTn value above the 99th percentile URL is defined as myocardial injury" (Section 5), and "sex-specific 99th percentile URLs are recommended for hs-cTn assays" (Section 25) — i.e. with sex-specific limits she has myocardial injury. Acute MI additionally requires "a rising and/or falling pattern of cTn values with at least one value above the 99th percentile URL" with clinical evidence of ischaemia (Section 5). The 2020 Clin Chem paper summarises the same document: "The Fourth Universal Definition of Myocardial Infarction (2018) recommended that the 99th percentile upper reference limit (URL) should not be used in isolation as a dichotomous test for MI diagnosis, but emphasized evaluating results in the context of the clinical presentation." [S4, S6]

- **Confidence**: **High** for the thresholds, trial numbers and guideline wording (all read from primary documents). **Medium** for the adoption statistics (England 2019 verified; US figure unverified).

### Creatinine and eGFR (with cystatin C)

- **Units**: creatinine mg/dL (US) and µmol/L (SI); 1 mg/dL = 88.4 µmol/L. eGFR in mL/min/1.73 m². Cystatin C in mg/L (same in both systems).

- **Typical lab reference range**:
  - Labcorp, Creatinine (test 001370), method "Kinetic Jaffe": ≥15 y **Female 0.57−1.00 mg/dL; Male 0.76−1.27 mg/dL** (children share one range by age band). Sex-specific: yes. Footnote: "Labcorp internal studies." https://www.labcorp.com/tests/001370/creatinine [S11]
  - ARUP, Creatinine, Serum or Plasma (0020025), "Quantitative Enzymatic Assay": reference interval "By report (reports may vary based on instrumentation)" — no published range. https://ltd.aruplab.com/Tests/Pub/0020025 [S12]
  - Labcorp, Cystatin C with eGFR (121265), "Turbidimetric (directly traceable to ERM-DA471/IFCC …)": no reference interval printed on the public page; eGFR "is calculated from a serum cystatin C using the CKD-EPI equation, 2012". https://www.labcorp.com/tests/121265/cystatin-c-with-glomerular-filtration-rate-estimated-egfr [S13]
  - Mayo Clinic Laboratories and Quest pages could not be read (HTTP 403 / JavaScript-only).

- **Where that range came from**: Labcorp attributes its sex-partitioned creatinine interval only to "Labcorp internal studies" (no population, n, or year given) [S11]; ARUP publishes none [S12]. The sex difference in creatinine itself is conventionally explained by muscle mass: in 170 healthy adults (92 women, 78 men), lean mass was independently associated with serum and urinary creatinine but not with cystatin C, and physically active subjects had higher creatinine (1.04 ± 0.12 vs 0.95 ± 0.17 mg/dL) with higher lean mass (55.3 ± 10.0% vs 48.5 ± 10.4%) [S16]. The eGFR equation encodes sex directly: CKD-EPI 2021 = 142 × min(Scr/κ, 1)^α × max(Scr/κ, 1)^−1.200 × 0.9938^Age × **1.012 [if female]**, with κ = 0.7 (female) / 0.9 (male) and α = −0.241 (female) / −0.302 (male) [S14, S15]. It was fitted on "10 studies (8254 participants, 31.5% Black)" for creatinine (3,606 [43.7%] female) and "13 studies (5352 participants, 39.7% Black)" for creatinine–cystatin C, validated in "12 studies (4050 participants, 14.3% Black)"; "Sex differences for eGFRcr and eGFRcr-cys equations are modeled as sex-specific creatinine coefficients as well as female sex coefficients." [S14]

- **Official sex-specific thresholds**: The sex terms of CKD-EPI 2021 (κ 0.7 vs 0.9; α −0.241 vs −0.302; multiplier 1.012 for women) [S14, S15]. CKD staging thresholds (eGFR <60) are not sex-specific. KDIGO 2024 Recommendation 1.2.2.1: "We recommend using eGFRcr-cys in clinical situations when eGFRcr is less accurate and GFR affects clinical decision-making (1C)." Figure 11 footnotes: "Initial test may be estimated GFR by cystatin C (eGFRcys or eGFRcr-cys) in otherwise healthy populations with changes in creatinine generation due to non-GFR determinants such as changes in muscle mass …"; "Consider eGFRcys rather than eGFRcr-cys in otherwise healthy populations with decreased creatinine generation due to reduced muscle mass …". [S17]

- **What is contested**: (1) The female creatinine "normal" range is a proprietary, undocumented internal interval at the largest US commercial lab [S11] and is not published at all by ARUP [S12]. (2) Creatinine tracks muscle mass, not only filtration: lean mass predicted creatinine but not cystatin C in healthy adults [S16], so a woman with low muscle mass can sit inside the female range while her GFR is reduced — which is exactly the situation KDIGO 2024 names as a reason to use cystatin C [S17]. (3) The sex coefficient in eGFR is a population average: the 2021 refit was driven by removing race, and the female multiplier (1.012) and sex-specific κ/α were re-estimated on a development set that was 43.7% female [S14]. No study was found in this session that directly quantified under-detection of CKD in women attributable to the female creatinine range; that specific claim should be treated as **unverified**.

- **What a reader with a value inside the contested band is told by the guidelines**: KDIGO 2024: "Use serum creatinine (SCr) and an estimating equation for initial assessment of GFR" (Practice Point 1.2.2.1) and "We recommend using eGFRcr-cys in clinical situations when eGFRcr is less accurate and GFR affects clinical decision-making" (Recommendation 1.2.2.1, 1C); "Interpretation of SCr level requires consideration of dietary intake" (Practice Point 1.2.2.4). [S17]

- **Confidence**: **High** for the equation coefficients and KDIGO wording; **Medium** for the provenance of the female lab range (documented only as "internal studies"); **Low** for any quantified under-detection claim in women.

### NT-proBNP / BNP

- **Units**: pg/mL (conventional) = ng/L (SI), factor 1. pmol/L conversion: NT-proBNP pg/mL × 0.118 = pmol/L; BNP pg/mL × 0.289 = pmol/L (conversion factors from memory — **unverified** in this session).

- **Typical lab reference range**: University of Iowa Hospitals pathology handbook, NT-proBNP (electrochemiluminescence immunoassay), pg/mL, adult ranges stated to be "95th percentiles in patients without congestive heart failure": Males 19–44 y 0–93; 45–54 y 0–138; 55–64 y 0–177; 65–74 y 0–229; ≥75 y 0–852. **Females 19–44 y 0–178; 45–54 y 0–192; 55–64 y 0–226; 65–74 y 0–353; ≥75 y 0–624.** Sex-specific: yes (women higher at every age below 75). The page also states an NT-proBNP "below 300 pg/mL effectively rules out acute CHF" and that "Levels may be falsely low in CHF patients with increased body mass index." https://www.healthcare.uiowa.edu/path_handbook/rhandbook/test2621.html [S18]. A Labcorp NT-proBNP page could not be located; Mayo's catalogue (which cites the Roche package insert 03/2024) returned 403.

- **Where that range came from**: The Iowa adult ranges are described only as 95th percentiles "in patients without CHF" (population not further specified) [S18]. The guideline cut-offs come from the ESC: "The upper limits of normal in the non-acute setting are 35 pg/mL for BNP, and 125 pg/mL for NT-proBNP." (ESC 2021 HF guideline, Section 4.2.1) [S19]. The largest sex-stratified healthy-population study is Welsh et al. 2022 (Generation Scotland, n = 18,356 without prior CVD, Roche assay): median (97.5th percentile) NT-proBNP, pg/mL — men <30 y 21 (104), 50–59 y 38 (195), ≥80 y 281 (6,792); **women <30 y 51 (196), 50–59 y 66 (299), ≥80 y 240 (2,704)** [S20].

- **Official sex-specific thresholds**: **None.** ESC 2021: "A plasma concentration of B-type natriuretic peptide (BNP) <35 pg/mL, N-terminal pro-B-type natriuretic peptide (NT-proBNP) <125 pg/mL, or mid-regional pro-atrial natriuretic peptide (MR-proANP) <40 pmol/L make a diagnosis of HF unlikely." Modifiers named by the ESC are "AF, increasing age, and acute or chronic kidney disease" and "NP concentrations may be disproportionately low in obese patients" — sex is not mentioned [S19]. (Acute-setting thresholds of NT-proBNP <300 and BNP <100 pg/mL are widely cited but were not located in the ESC text read in this session — **unverified** here.)

- **What is contested**: The single 125 pg/mL rule-out is set without regard to sex, yet healthy women run higher. In Generation Scotland, NT-proBNP ≥125 pg/mL was found in **9.8% of women vs 1.4% of men under 30**, and in 76.5% of women vs 81.0% of men aged ≥80; after adjustment, women were far more likely to exceed 125 pg/mL (OR 9.48, 95% CI 5.60–16.1). The authors: "An NT-proBNP ≥125 pg/mL is common in females without classical cardiovascular risk factors as well as older people", and age- and sex-specific thresholds will be needed if NT-proBNP is used for screening [S20]. In ARIC (12,750 at midlife, 5,191 in late life; Myhre et al. 2022), NT-proBNP was lowest in Black men (median 30 pg/mL midlife, 74 late life) and highest in White women (70 and 154 pg/mL); at guideline-recommended thresholds the rate ratio for HF or death was 6.7 (4.6–9.9) at midlife and 2.7 (1.7–4.1) in late life, with "higher risk consistently observed among Black men and lower risk in White women" [S21]. Direction of the problem: a uniform threshold over-flags healthy women (and may under-detect Black men); this is the opposite direction from troponin.

- **What a reader with a value inside the contested band is told by the guidelines** (e.g. a woman with NT-proBNP 125–300 pg/mL): ESC 2021 — "Plasma concentrations of NPs are recommended as initial diagnostic tests" and a value <125 pg/mL makes HF "unlikely"; above that the guideline directs to echocardiography, noting elevated NPs have non-cardiac causes including "AF, increasing age, and acute or chronic kidney disease" (Section 4.2, Table 7) [S19].

- **Confidence**: **Medium-high**. ESC thresholds, Welsh and Myhre numbers are from primary sources; a Labcorp/Mayo/Quest page could not be opened, and the acute cut-offs were not verified.

### Creatine kinase (CK)

- **Units**: U/L (= IU/L) conventional; SI µkat/L = U/L × 0.0167.

- **Typical lab reference range**: Labcorp, Creatine Kinase (CK), Total (001362), method "Kinetic − 340 nm spectrophotometric": **18–50 y Male 49−439 U/L; Female 32−182 U/L**; 51–80 y Male 41–331, Female 32–182; >80 y Male 30–208, Female 26–161. Sex-specific: yes. No source footnote is attached to the table. https://www.labcorp.com/tests/001362/creatine-kinase-ck-total [S22]

- **Where that range came from**: Not stated by Labcorp [S22]. Independently derived population limits (97.5th percentile):
  - Tromsø Study, Norway (Lilleng et al., Clin Chem Lab Med 2012): 6,904 analysed after excluding 5,924 with possible causes of hyperCKaemia; URL **women 207 U/L; men <50 y 395 U/L; men ≥50 y 340 U/L**; women and younger men "in accordance with" the Nordic Reference Interval Project (NORIP), older men higher [S23].
  - Amsterdam SUNSET study (Brewster et al., Am Heart J 2007): 1,444 individuals aged 34–60 (503 white European, 292 South Asian, 580 African descent), sampled "after 3 days of rest"; the calculated URLs for women and men were "2 to 5 times higher than recommended by the assay manufacturer", and "13% of the white Europeans, 23% of South Asians, and 49% of the black people" exceeded the manufacturer's limits. Exact per-sex values are in the full text, which was not opened [S24].
  - NHANES 2011–2014 (George et al., Medicine 2016): 10,096 non-pregnant US adults ≥20 y, excluding recent strenuous exercise; 97.5th percentiles **white men 382, Black men 1,001, white women 295, Black women 487 U/L**; Black race strongly associated with CK (OR 5.08 Black women, 8.39 Black men); "Body composition explained the age-related differences but not the racial differences" [S25].

- **Official sex-specific thresholds**: No society guideline sets a sex-specific CK diagnostic cut-off; practice uses the laboratory's sex-partitioned URL (and multiples of it, e.g. for statin myopathy). NORIP limits quoted in indexing records for the companion Tromsø paper (210 U/L women, 400 U/L men <50, 280 U/L men ≥50) were seen in search snippets only — **unverified** [S26].

- **What is contested**: The female "normal" upper limit in common use (Labcorp 182 U/L [S22]) sits below every population-derived 97.5th percentile for women: 207 U/L in Norway [S23], 295 U/L in white and 487 U/L in Black US women [S25]. Ancestry matters more than sex-partitioning alone: nearly half (49%) of people of African descent exceeded the manufacturer's limit in Amsterdam [S24]. A healthy Black woman therefore has a substantial chance of a "high" CK on a standard report, with the attendant risk of statin discontinuation or unnecessary neuromuscular work-up — the stated motivation of Brewster et al. ("Eligible subjects with mildly elevated serum creatine kinase (CK) activity are often excluded" from statin trials) [S24].

- **What a reader with a value inside the contested band is told by the guidelines**: No guideline text on CK interpretation by sex was opened in this session; Brewster et al. conclude "The variation in CK activity within the population is wider than previously suggested" and that raising the URL "may improve the use of statins" [S24]. Mark guideline guidance as **unverified**.

- **Confidence**: **Medium**. Lab range and NHANES/Tromsø percentiles verified; Brewster per-sex values and NORIP limits not read directly.

### Uric acid / urate

- **Units**: mg/dL (conventional) and µmol/L (SI); 1 mg/dL = 59.48 µmol/L (6 mg/dL ≈ 360 µmol/L; 6.8 mg/dL ≈ 405 µmol/L; 7 mg/dL ≈ 420 µmol/L).

- **Typical lab reference range**: Labcorp, Uric Acid (001057), method "Uricase": **Male 18 years and older: 3.8–8.4 mg/dL**; **Female 18 to 50 years: 2.6–6.2 mg/dL; 51 to 70 years: 3.0–7.2 mg/dL; 71 years and older: 3.1–7.9 mg/dL.** Sex-specific: yes (and age-partitioned for women only). The page also prints "Therapeutic target for gout patients: <6.02" [mg/dL]. The male interval carries the footnote "LabCorp internal studies"; the female interval carries no source footnote. https://www.labcorp.com/tests/001057/uric-acid [S27]

- **Where that range came from**: Undocumented for women at Labcorp [S27]. The widely reproduced pair "men 3.4–7.0 / women 2.4–5.7 mg/dL" appears to originate in a German population study of 1,183 adults (443 men, 740 women) using an enzymatic colour test — seen only in a search snippet (Thieme PDF blocked) — **unverified** [S31]. The gout thresholds have a physical-chemistry origin: the monosodium urate "saturation threshold at 37°C is 6.8 mg/dl (408 µmol/l)" (Perez-Ruiz, Rheumatology 2009 — snippet only, **unverified** [S32]); the ACR guideline defines asymptomatic hyperuricemia "as an SU concentration of ≥6.8 mg/dl with no prior gout flares or subcutaneous tophi" [S28].

- **Official sex-specific thresholds**: **None** in the treatment guidelines. ACR 2020: "we strongly recommend continuing ULT to achieve and maintain an SU target of <6 mg/dl over no target" [S28]. EULAR 2016: serum urate "<6 mg/dL (360 µmol/L) and <5 mg/dL (300 µmol/L) in those with severe gout" [S29]. Both apply to men and women alike; the only sex-partitioning is in laboratory reference intervals [S27].

- **What is contested**: The female lab "normal" is set from an (undocumented) healthy-population distribution, not from disease risk, and it collides with the disease thresholds: Labcorp's female upper limits of 7.2 mg/dL (51–70 y) and 7.9 mg/dL (≥71 y) sit above the ACR/EULAR treatment target of 6 mg/dL and above the 6.8 mg/dL saturation-based definition of hyperuricaemia [S27, S28, S29]. In the Framingham cohort (Bhole et al. 2010; 2,476 women, 1,951 men, 1950–2002; 304 incident gout cases, 104 in women) gout risk in women rose in a graded manner with serum urate, though less steeply than in men [S30]; the category-specific incidences (0.8, 2.5, 4.2, 13.1 and 27.3 per 1,000 person-years for <5.0, 5.0–5.9, 6.0–6.9, 7.0–7.9 and ≥8.0 mg/dL) were seen only in a search summary — **unverified**. The ACR guideline itself acknowledges "Gout has differential impact on patients by sex, race, or by presence of other comorbidities" but its patient panel was "8 male patients with gout" [S28].

- **What a reader with a value inside the contested band is told by the guidelines** (e.g. a woman with urate 6.0–6.8 mg/dL, "normal" on the lab report but above the treatment target): ACR 2020 — "Initiating ULT is conditionally recommended against in patients with asymptomatic hyperuricemia" (defined as ≥6.8 mg/dl without flares or tophi) [S28]; for a person with diagnosed gout, "an SU target of <6 mg/dl" is strongly recommended [S28], and EULAR likewise "<6 mg/dL (360 µmol/L)" [S29]. In other words, below 6.8 and without gout, no action; with gout, the lab's "normal" is irrelevant and <6 is the target.

- **Confidence**: **Medium**. Lab ranges and guideline targets verified; the historical source of the female interval and the Framingham category-level incidences are unverified.
